# Model Linguistics — Core Research Direction

Date: 2026-10-26
Status: Working hypothesis, not established mechanism.

## Core Hypothesis

Grammar may be best understood as coordinated relational
computation rather than a fixed internal grammatical
representation.

A neural language model may transform distributed linguistic
states into organized relational information through
context-dependent computation.

The central scientific target is the transformation itself,
not necessarily a universal grammar vector.

## Research Objective

Discover computational principles by which language models:

1. Interpret linguistic dependencies.
2. Resolve participants and their relationships.
3. Coordinate information across positions and layers.
4. Preserve relational meaning across different constructions.
5. Make that information available for question-conditioned
   interpretation and answer selection.

## Evidence from ML-020 to ML-025

- Two relational dependencies can be independently manipulated.
- Correct within-pair relational sensitivity can coexist with
  incorrect global answer selection.
- Early cue-position activation patches closely reproduce input
  edits, but later cue-position effects are less portable.
- Causal influence depends on linguistic construction and
  contextual arrangement.
- ML-025 weakened a simple grammatical-role explanation of
  the layer-7 effects observed in ML-024.

These results motivate the coordination hypothesis but do
not establish it.

## Competing Explanations

- Lexical matching and name accessibility
- Surface position and word-order heuristics
- Tokenization and candidate likelihood biases
- Question-conditioned retrieval
- Context-dependent distributed computation

## Next Research Stage

ML-026: Coordinated Relational Transformation

Primary objective:
Identify a predictive computation that explains relational
equivalence across linguistic constructions.

Required tests:
- Semantically equivalent constructions
- Counterfactual semantic reversals
- Held-out names, predicates, and constructions
- Lexical and positional controls
- Causal validation against non-relational baselines

Success requires prospective prediction and causal evidence,
not merely an interpretable activation pattern.

## Scientific Principle

Do not force evidence to support the coordination hypothesis.

Find the computation that best explains the observations,
including evidence that contradicts our preferred theory.
